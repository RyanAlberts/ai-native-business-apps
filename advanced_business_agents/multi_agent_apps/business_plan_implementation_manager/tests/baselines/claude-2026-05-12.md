## Day 0–30: Foundation

*Theme: The machine is loaded before we pull the trigger — list built, integration stable in staging, offer crisp, outreach live.*

1. **Build the 200-agency HawkSoft prospect list**
   Acceptance: Spreadsheet with 200 rows; each row has agency name, owner name, direct email, HawkSoft AMS confirmed (self-identified in profile/website), at least one commercial-lines indicator (carrier appointment pages, "commercial" in services copy, or LinkedIn).
   Effort: ~16 hrs (manual research + Apollo/LinkedIn enrichment)

2. **HawkSoft integration staging-stable**
   Acceptance: Bidirectional sync (contacts, accounts, policies in; activity log back) runs 3 consecutive days with zero failures; data fidelity QA checklist signed off by the founder.
   Effort: ~40 hrs eng

3. **Commercial lines account hierarchy: v1 in staging**
   Acceptance: Named insured + additional insureds + certificate holders can be linked to a single parent commercial account. A non-engineer can configure a real account end-to-end without help.
   Effort: ~30 hrs eng

4. **Sales package complete**
   Acceptance: One-pager readable in under 60 seconds; 5-touch outbound email sequence loaded and personalized to HawkSoft ICP; demo script lets founder run a 30-minute call without slides; pricing page live.
   Effort: ~12 hrs

5. **Cold outreach machine live — first 40 emails out**
   Acceptance: Email tool configured (Instantly, Apollo, or Smartlead), deliverability warmed, first 40 personalized emails sent to ICP targets. Open rate ≥35% on warm-up batch.
   Effort: ~8 hrs setup + ~8 hrs first-week execution

6. **3 discovery calls booked**
   Acceptance: 3 calendar invites from HawkSoft agencies on the books before Day 30; call notes template ready in CRM.
   Effort: Incremental ~4 hrs follow-up on top of outreach above

7. **Onboarding runbook v1**
   Acceptance: Step-by-step doc — HawkSoft credentials → sync config → first account import → user provisioning — that a non-founder can execute in under 2 hours.
   Effort: ~8 hrs

## Day 31–60: Validation

*Theme: Real customers on the product; every strategic assumption gets a data point.*

1. **Close 3 paying customers ($1,347 MRR)**
   Acceptance: 3 signed contracts + first invoice collected in Stripe. All 3 onboarded per the runbook.
   Effort: ~60 hrs (40 emails/wk × 4 wks, discovery calls, demos, close, onboarding)

2. **HawkSoft integration in production — 14-day clean window**
   Acceptance: Zero Sev-1 incidents (data loss, duplicate records, missed policy pulls) across the live customer base in any 14-day window. Any incident triggers an RCA within 24 hrs.
   Effort: ~20 hrs eng (hardening, monitoring setup, on-call response)

3. **Commercial lines hierarchy in active production use by 2 customers**
   Acceptance: 2 of the 3 paying customers each have ≥5 real commercial accounts configured with linked contacts — verified in product analytics, not self-reported.
   Effort: ~10 hrs customer success + bug fixes

4. **Customer discovery interviews: all live accounts**
   Acceptance: 30-minute structured call with each customer; NPS collected; written summary per call with top 3 objections, top 3 delights, and 1 feature request. Target NPS ≥40.
   Effort: ~8 hrs

5. **Objection library from calls**
   Acceptance: ≥15 distinct objections catalogued with the response that worked or didn't, rated by win/loss outcome. Demo script updated accordingly.
   Effort: ~4 hrs synthesis (ongoing, not a block)

6. **3 "community connector" customers identified and prioritized**
   Acceptance: Among live customers, flag those active in Insurance Nerds, Big "I" Young Agents Slack, or HawkSoft User Group. Each connector has a named owner (the founder), a logged NPS ≥60, and has been asked — not told — whether they'd post honestly about their experience if they wanted to.
   Effort: ~6 hrs relationship work

7. **First Big "I" chapter or cluster group meeting booked**
   Acceptance: One actual video or in-person meeting with a named decision-maker at SIAA, Keystone, or a state Big "I" chapter scheduled before Day 60 ends. Routed through a warm intro from a customer or community contact if possible.
   Effort: ~12 hrs research + outreach

## Day 61–90: Scaling decision

*Theme: The experiments that determine whether to double down, hire, or change the motion.*

1. **Reach 10 paying customers (≥$4,490 MRR)**
   Acceptance: Stripe shows ≥$4,490 MRR; all 10 agencies live on the integration; none on a trial or deferred billing arrangement.
   Effort: ~80 hrs (40 emails/wk + calls + closes + onboarding)

2. **30-day zero-critical-failure integration window**
   Acceptance: No Sev-1 incident across the full customer base in the 30-day window ending Day 90. This is Objective 2 made concrete.
   Effort: ~15 hrs monitoring, on-call, and response

3. **3 unsolicited community endorsements live**
   Acceptance: 3 organic posts from agency owners in HawkSoft User Group, Big "I" Young Agents Slack, Insurance Nerds, or equivalent — captured (screenshot + URL). None prompted by a direct ask from the founder.
   Effort: 0 hrs direct; ~10 hrs community monitoring to catch and log them

4. **Commercial hierarchy: 3 customers actively using it**
   Acceptance: 3 paying customers each managing ≥10 commercial accounts with linked contacts in production. Verified in analytics.
   Effort: ~10 hrs customer success

5. **Big "I" / cluster group: substantive conversation underway**
   Acceptance: The Day 60 meeting happened; follow-up sent; a second meeting or member-pilot discussion is in a live email thread with a named decision-maker.
   Effort: ~8 hrs (meeting prep, follow-up, next-step pursuit)

6. **Unit economics audit**
   Acceptance: One-page dashboard with actual CAC (founder hours × imputed rate ÷ customers closed), payback period, MRR, and Week-4 product engagement rate per cohort. No projections — actuals only.
   Effort: ~6 hrs data pull + write-up

7. **Second-90-days plan drafted and decided**
   Acceptance: Written decision on three questions: (a) when to hire the first sales rep (or not), (b) whether to begin EZLynx integration, (c) whether to open a community/content channel. Each decision cites the data from the unit economics audit.
   Effort: ~8 hrs strategic synthesis

## Critical decisions / forks

**Fork 1: Hire a sales rep, or stay founder-led?**
Decision-able: Day 75
Criterion: If conversion rate ≥30% and the bottleneck is clearly outreach *volume* (pipeline full, close rate healthy, founder spread thin), hire. If conversion <20%, the message isn't working — no hire until it is. Adding headcount to a broken funnel compounds the failure.
Branch A (hire): +$5–8k/month burn; 30-day ramp lag before productive; founder reclaims time for product and distribution strategy.
Branch B (stay solo): Growth ceiling constrained by founder hours; deeper first-call learning; preserves runway through a period when product risk is still real.

**Fork 2: Add EZLynx integration, or deepen HawkSoft?**
Decision-able: Day 80
Criterion: If ≥3 lost deals in the pipeline explicitly cited EZLynx as a hard blocker (not a preference), begin the build. Otherwise, invest that engineering time in HawkSoft depth — faster sync, broader field mapping, audit log, error surfacing.
Branch A (EZLynx): Opens a second TAM segment; 6–8 weeks of eng; splits QA surface area and creates integration maintenance debt.
Branch B (HawkSoft depth): Deepens the moat with the beachhead; no new integration risk; compresses the time to the 30-day zero-failure window.

**Fork 3: Pursue the cluster group deal or keep it warm?**
Decision-able: Day 85
Criterion: If the Big "I"/SIAA/Keystone conversation has a named champion, an internal timeline, and a next step the counterparty owns — invest 20% of founder time in it. If it's vague, no champion, no timeline — park it until Day 150 and don't let it crowd the direct sales motion.
Branch A (pursue): Potential for 10–50 agencies in one deal; long cycle; high distraction risk during the month you're trying to close customers 8–10.
Branch B (keep warm): Seed planted; no distraction; small risk of a competitor moving faster if they're closer to this community than you are.

**Fork 4: Open a content/community channel or stay pure outbound?**
Decision-able: Day 85
Criterion: If ≥2 inbound leads arrived *without* a direct referral from an existing customer — organic search, forum post, or cold inbound — the channel is self-seeding and worth 4–6 hrs/week. Otherwise, the reference base isn't large enough yet to make content credible. Stay outbound.
Branch A (add content): Compounds slowly; requires 3–6 months of consistency before meaningful return; risks founder attention fragmentation.
Branch B (stay outbound): Predictable, founder-controlled, and well-matched to a 10-customer reference base that isn't yet large enough to validate every claim you'd make in public.

## Owner & cadence

**Owner:** Founder, across all tracks. This is a solo execution machine until the Fork 1 hire decision resolves.

**Weekly cadence:**

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| **Monday** | 30-minute roadmap review. What was supposed to ship this week? On track? What moves? |
| **Wednesday** | Outbound batch sent (40 emails). Check open rate, reply rate, calls booked. |
| **Friday** | MRR and pipeline update: deals closed, deals lost, one-line diagnosis on each loss. |

**Monthly (end of each 30-day block):** Full metrics dashboard reviewed against targets. Written one-paragraph diagnosis — what's ahead of plan, what's behind, what's changing as a result. No exceptions. The founder who skips the monthly review is flying blind into the Fork 1 decision.

The roadmap anchors are the six strategic objectives. Every deliverable above maps to at least one. If a task doesn't trace back to those six, it doesn't belong in the next 90 days.
