## Federal filings (annual)

- **Form 1040 + Schedule C** (sole proprietorship profit/loss) — due **April 15**. Extension to October 15 via Form 4868, but that is an extension to *file*, **not to pay** — any tax owed is still due April 15. [IRS small-business resources](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed)
- **Schedule SE** (self-employment tax) — attached to your 1040. SE tax is **15.3%** on net earnings up to the Social Security wage base (approximately $176,100 for 2025; verify the 2026 limit), then **2.9%** Medicare above that. At $80k revenue with ~$65–70k net income, budget roughly **$10,500–$11,000 in SE tax alone**, before federal income tax.
- **Form 1040-ES** (quarterly estimated taxes) — due **June 15 · September 15 · January 15 · April 15**. ⚠️ Q2 is due **June 15, 2026 — 16 days from today.** Pay free at IRS Direct Pay or register for EFTPS (recommended for recurring payments). A safe harbor: pay 100% of last year's tax liability in equal quarterly installments and you owe no underpayment penalty regardless of this year's actual income.
- **EIN (Employer Identification Number)** — technically optional for a sole prop with no employees, but strongly recommended. Using an EIN instead of your SSN on client contracts and any future 1099s you receive protects your Social Security number. [Apply free in 5 minutes at the IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online).
- **Form 1099-NEC** — **not required** (no contractors paid $600+).
- **Forms W-2 / W-3 / 940 / 941** — **not required** (no employees).
- **FinCEN BOI filing** — **not required.** Under FinCEN's interim final rule effective March 26, 2025, entities formed in the United States are **exempt** from Beneficial Ownership Information reporting. As a US-based sole proprietor, you owe no BOI filing and should not pay any service to file one on your behalf. This rule has changed multiple times — verify current status at the [FinCEN BOI FAQ](https://www.fincen.gov/boi-faqs).

## Bookkeeping setup

**Recommended tool: [Wave](https://www.waveapps.com/) — free**

At $80k revenue with no employees, no inventory, and a straightforward service model, Wave is the right tool. It handles invoicing, expense tracking, bank/card import, and basic P&L reporting at no cost. When you eventually hire a CPA or bookkeeper, Wave has accountant-access sharing built in. If your CPA insists on QuickBooks, migrating at that point is a one-time afternoon of work.

**Supporting practices:**

- **Open a business checking account now.** Mercury and Relay are both free, no-minimum online business accounts. Never run coaching income or business expenses through your personal account — clean separation is the single biggest gift you can give yourself at tax time.
- **Get a dedicated business credit card.** Even a simple 2% cash-back card creates a clean paper trail for every expense. Pay it in full monthly.
- **Reconcile monthly, not at year-end.** Year-end catch-up bookkeeping costs 3–5× more in CPA hours and makes deduction review stressful.
- **Home-office deduction — document it now.** If you use part of your home exclusively and regularly for coaching (client calls, admin, session prep), you can deduct that portion of rent/mortgage interest, utilities, and internet on Schedule C. Measure the square footage of your workspace vs. your total home and keep that note on file.
- **Health insurance deduction.** As a sole proprietor, you can deduct 100% of health insurance premiums for yourself and your family directly on Schedule 1 (Form 1040) — not on Schedule C, but just as valuable. Track every premium paid.
- **SEP-IRA.** You can contribute up to 25% of net SE income (roughly $15–18k at this revenue level) to a SEP-IRA by your tax-filing deadline, including extensions. That is a dollar-for-dollar reduction in taxable income. Open the account at Fidelity, Vanguard, or Schwab — all free with no minimums — before December 31 of the tax year you want to use.
- **Mileage.** If you drive to client meetings, networking events, or workshops, track business miles. IRS standard rate for 2025 was $0.70/mile; verify the 2026 rate. A free app like MileIQ or Everlance takes 10 seconds per trip.

## Common pitfalls

- **Missing the June 15 estimated tax payment.** It is 16 days away. The federal underpayment penalty is modest per quarter (~3–5% annualized) but adds up, and an unexpected $20k+ tax bill in April hits hard. Pay now, even an approximate amount — you can true up later.
- **Not opening a separate business bank account.** Mingling personal and business transactions makes every deduction arguable under audit and turns bookkeeping into archaeology. This is a Day 1 task, not a someday task.
- **Skipping the SEP-IRA.** Most first-year service founders miss this entirely, leaving $3,000–$5,000 in avoidable taxes on the table. You have until your filing deadline (including extension) to contribute for a given tax year — but the account must be opened before December 31.
- **Ignoring the home-office deduction.** The "exclusive and regular use" test is real but not hard to satisfy if you have a dedicated workspace. Founders routinely skip this because it "feels aggressive." It's a legitimate deduction that Congress specifically created for self-employed people working from home.
- **Assuming the FinCEN BOI filing still applies.** Under the 2025 interim final rule, US-formed entities are exempt. Don't pay a compliance service to file it — that advice is out of date. Check the [FinCEN FAQ](https://www.fincen.gov/boi-faqs) for current status.

## Federal filings (annual)

- **Form 1120 (C-Corp income tax return)**: Due **April 15** for calendar-year corporations. A 6-month extension is available via **Form 7004** (extends filing to October 15) — but the extension does **not** extend time to pay; taxes owed still accrue interest from April 15. File through your CPA. → [IRS small-business resources](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed)

- **Form 1099-NEC** (US contractor): Due **January 31** for any US-based contractor paid $600+ during the year. Issue to recipient and file Copy A with the IRS simultaneously. File electronically via IRS FIRE system or a payroll service like Gusto. → [IRS small-business resources](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed)

- **W-8BEN (Brazil & Canada contractors)**: Not an IRS filing, but a critical document you collect and retain. Each foreign contractor (individuals) must provide a completed **Form W-8BEN** before their first payment. Entities provide **W-8BEN-E**. These establish their foreign status and exempt you from 1099-NEC issuance and 30% backup withholding. Keep these on file — they expire after 3 years.

- **Form 1042 / 1042-S** ⚠️: This is the annual return for US-source income paid to foreign persons. Payments to your Brazil/Canada contractors for software development services **performed entirely outside the US** are generally **not US-source income** and not subject to withholding — meaning Form 1042 may not be required. However, this analysis depends on the nature and location of services. Have your CPA confirm your 1042/1042-S obligation specifically.

- **No Form W-2 / W-3**: Not required — you have no W-2 employees.

- **No Forms 940 / 941**: Not required — no payroll.

- **Corporate estimated taxes (Form 1120-W)**: C-Corps pay quarterly estimated corporate income taxes — these are **not** Form 1040-ES (that's for individuals and pass-through entities). Deadlines for calendar-year corps: **April 15, June 15, September 15, December 15**. ⚡ Your next deadline is **June 15, 2026 — 16 days away.** At 21% federal corporate rate on $1.2M revenue with meaningful margins, the underpayment penalty can add up fast. Get your CPA on this immediately.

- **BOI filing with FinCEN**: **Not required.** Under FinCEN's interim final rule effective March 26, 2025, entities formed in the United States are **exempt** from Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reporting. Your Delaware C-Corp is US-formed, so no filing is currently required. This rule has changed multiple times — verify current status at the [FinCEN BOI FAQ](https://www.fincen.gov/boi-faqs) before relying on this exemption.

## Common pitfalls

1. **Delaware franchise tax shock**: Opening a $50,000–$100,000 Delaware franchise tax bill because the Assumed Par Value Capital method wasn't elected. The default authorized-shares method is designed for shell companies with minimal assets — it produces absurd results for real operating companies. Always elect APVC at [corp.delaware.gov/paytaxes](https://corp.delaware.gov/paytaxes/).

2. **No foreign qualification in your home state**: Operating out of California, New York, Texas, or anywhere other than Delaware without registering as a foreign corporation means you cannot enforce contracts in that state's courts. Lawyers and CPAs see this constantly. Identify your home state and fix this in the next 30 days.

3. **Treating foreign contractors like domestic ones**: Issuing 1099-NECs to your Brazil or Canada contractors is incorrect and creates a paper trail the IRS can use to assess liability. Use W-8BEN forms. Also confirm with your CPA whether Form 1042 is required — the analysis depends on where the services are physically performed.

4. **Missing corporate estimated tax payments**: C-Corp estimated taxes are **not** the same as personal estimated taxes (1040-ES). The C-Corp pays them directly at the corporate level on April 15, June 15, September 15, and December 15. At 21% federal rate on meaningful margins, skipping a quarterly payment generates a penalty that compounds. Your **June 15 payment is 16 days away**.

5. **Assuming BOI reporting is still required**: Under FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule, US-formed entities are **exempt** from BOI reporting. You do not owe this filing. Anyone telling you that you owe a $500/day penalty for not filing BOI is giving you outdated advice. Verify current status at the [FinCEN BOI FAQ](https://www.fincen.gov/boi-faqs).
