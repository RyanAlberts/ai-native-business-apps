# Tax & Regulatory Compliance Plan
**Delaware LLC · California Skincare E-Commerce · Shopify + Amazon FBA · ~$200k Year 1**

---

## Recommendation summary

Three things deserve your attention right now, in this order. First, **register as a foreign LLC in California** — you have physical operations there (3PL, and presumably you're managing the business from CA), which makes you "doing business" in the state under California law. Operating without registration exposes you to back taxes, penalties, and loss of the ability to enforce contracts in CA courts. Second, **audit your Amazon FBA warehouse footprint immediately** — Amazon distributes your inventory across fulfillment centers in potentially 15–25 states, each one creating physical nexus for your *Shopify* sales tax obligations. Amazon handles its own platform as a marketplace facilitator, but your Shopify store is entirely on you, and this gap catches most FBA + DTC hybrid sellers off guard. Third, **file your BOI report with FinCEN** if you haven't yet — penalties are up to $500/day and the filing takes 15 minutes; verify current status at FinCEN.gov given ongoing litigation, but don't assume you're protected without checking.

## Sales tax nexus analysis

### Physical nexus states

**California** — Your CA-based 3PL creates definite, immediate physical nexus. Highest-priority state for registration and collection.

**Amazon FBA warehouse states (variable — must verify)** — Amazon's network means your inventory likely sits in fulfillment centers across a large swath of the country. Each state where Amazon stores *your* inventory = physical nexus for your Shopify sales to customers in that state. Amazon commonly uses FCs in:

> AZ · CA · CO · CT · FL · GA · IL · IN · KY · MD · MI · MN · NJ · NV · NY · NC · OH · PA · TN · TX · VA · WA · WI *(and potentially more — list evolves as Amazon opens warehouses)*

**Action**: In Amazon Seller Central → Reports → Fulfillment → Inventory → **Monthly Inventory History Report**. Filter by state. That list becomes your physical nexus map.

*States you can ignore even if Amazon has FCs there: Oregon, Montana, New Hampshire, Delaware — no state sales tax.*

---

### Economic nexus states

With $200k split between Amazon and Shopify across 50 states, your per-state Shopify revenue is likely modest. Most states use a **$100,000 in sales *or* 200 transactions** threshold (verify per state — thresholds and conjunctions vary):

| State | Economic nexus threshold | Note |
|---|---|---|
| California | $500,000 | Already have physical nexus — moot point |
| Texas | $500,000 | Higher bar — watch as you scale |
| Most other states | $100,000 or 200 transactions | Track Shopify sales by state monthly |

**Practical implication**: If your Shopify revenue is ~$50–100k and spread across all 50 states, you're likely under economic nexus thresholds in most states *except* where you already have FBA physical nexus. But you must track this monthly — crossing a threshold mid-year creates an obligation from that point forward.

---

### Marketplace facilitator states

**Amazon sales**: Amazon collects and remits sales tax as a marketplace facilitator in virtually every state with a sales tax. You do **not** collect or remit for Amazon-platform sales. ✅

**Important caveat**: Amazon's facilitator obligation covers *Amazon* sales only. It does **not** eliminate your obligation to register, collect, and remit sales tax on your **Shopify sales** in states where you have physical nexus from FBA inventory. This is the most commonly missed item for FBA + Shopify sellers.

Some states also require you to file returns (even zero-balance returns) once you're registered — confirm requirements per state when you register.

**Shopify**: Your Shopify store has no built-in facilitator protection. You are fully responsible for collection and remittance. Use **TaxJar** or **Avalara** to automate this — do not track manually across 15+ states.

## Common pitfalls

- **Assuming Amazon handles all your sales tax** — Amazon's marketplace facilitator role covers only Amazon-platform sales. Your Shopify sales into FBA-nexus states are entirely your responsibility to register, collect, and remit. This is the #1 compliance gap for FBA + DTC sellers.

- **Operating in California without foreign LLC registration** — California's Franchise Tax Board actively pursues unregistered foreign entities. Back taxes, penalties, interest, and the inability to enforce contracts in CA courts are all on the table. The $70 registration fee and $800 annual tax are non-negotiable.

- **Missing quarterly estimated tax payments** — As a pass-through LLC, your business income flows to your personal 1040. The IRS expects you to pay taxes as you earn. If you skip quarterly estimates, you'll owe an underpayment penalty *on top of* your tax bill. Set aside 25–30% of net profit in a separate savings account each month.

- **Buying inventory or ads on a personal card** — Even one or two personal-card business purchases creates bookkeeping complications that compound over the year. Open a business card immediately and use it exclusively for business spending.

- **Waiting until January to collect W-9s** — If you pay any designer, photographer, developer, or marketing freelancer $600+ during the year, you need their W-9 before you can issue a 1099-NEC. Contractors sometimes disappear or become unresponsive. Collect the W-9 the moment you engage them.

- **Skincare-specific — FDA MoCRA compliance** — The Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act requires cosmetics brands to register facilities and list products with the FDA. Failure to comply is a regulatory risk (not a tax issue), but it's real and enforceable. Confirm your product line's classification (cosmetic vs. drug/OTC) with a regulatory attorney.

## State Business Registrations

## Common Pitfalls

| **Form 1120** (C-Corp income tax return) | April 15; extension to October 15 | File Form 7004 by April 15 to extend. At $1.2M revenue, you almost certainly owe corporate income tax — do not let this slip. |
| **Corporate estimated taxes** (via EFTPS) | April 15, June 15, Sept 15, Dec 15 | C-Corps pay estimated taxes quarterly using EFTPS — this is NOT Form 1040-ES (that's for individuals/pass-throughs). Underpayment penalty applies. |

| **Form 1042 + 1042-S** (foreign contractors) | March 15 (1042-S to recipients); March 15 (1042 to IRS) | Required for payments to your Brazil and Canada contractors that constitute US-source income. Collect **W-8BEN** from each foreign contractor before any payment. |
| **30% withholding — Brazil contractor** | Ongoing with each payment | Brazil has **no income tax treaty with the US**. Payments for services performed in the US (or services that are US-sourced) may be subject to 30% withholding. If this contractor works exclusively in Brazil on work that is foreign-sourced, withholding may not apply — but this determination requires CPA sign-off. Do not assume you're clear. |
| **Canada contractor** | Ongoing | The US-Canada tax treaty generally reduces or eliminates withholding. Still collect W-8BEN and document the treaty position. |
| **BOI Filing (FinCEN)** | See note | The Corporate Transparency Act has been subject to court injunctions and regulatory changes through 2024–2025. **Verify the current status at fincen.gov** immediately — but if your filing window is open and you haven't filed, do it today. Penalties can reach $500/day. |
| **Form W-2 / W-3** | January 31 | Not applicable — no W-2 employees today |

## State Filings (Annual)

### Delaware
| Obligation | Deadline | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual report + franchise tax** | **March 1** | This is a hard deadline with no grace period. The **Authorized Shares method** can produce wildly inflated franchise tax bills (tens of thousands of dollars for a VC-backed cap table). Use the **Assumed Par Value Capital (APVC) method** instead — for most early-stage companies it's dramatically lower, often $400–$2,000. Your registered agent or CPA files this. |
| **Delaware state income tax** | Generally not applicable | Delaware imposes corporate income tax only on income *sourced* to Delaware. A SaaS company incorporated in DE but with no DE operations typically owes no DE income tax. Verify with CPA given your specific fact pattern. |

### States Where You Register for Sales Tax
| Obligation | Cadence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales tax returns** | Monthly or quarterly (assigned at registration) | Even $0 returns typically required. Late filings attract penalties. Automate with Avalara, TaxJar, or Anrok (purpose-built for SaaS). |

### State of US Contractor's Residence
Depending on which state and whether that contractor's relationship ever re-characterizes as employment, you may owe state income tax filings there. Flag for CPA review.

> 📌 **Honest caveat:** Specific state deadlines for annual reports in any state you eventually foreign-qualify in vary — verify each on the relevant Secretary of State or DOR site before filing.

## Bookkeeping Setup

**Recommended tool: QuickBooks Online (QBO)**

At $1.2M revenue with a Delaware C-Corp, international contractor payments, and real sales tax obligations, you need a platform your CPA and bookkeeper can access seamlessly. QBO is the de facto standard — virtually every US CPA and bookkeeper operates in it natively, it integrates with Stripe/Chargebee for SaaS revenue recognition, and it handles multi-currency for your Brazil and Canada contractor payments. The ~$85–$200/month cost is negligible at your revenue level.

> Alternative worth noting: **Xero** is a strong second choice if you prefer a cleaner UI or have global team members who need access — it also handles multi-currency well.

**Supporting practices:**

- [ ] **Dedicated business bank account and credit card** — if you're mixing personal and business finances, stop today. Mercury is excellent for startups (no fees, API access, virtual cards)
- [ ] **Reconcile monthly** — not at year-end. Catching mis-categorized contractor payments 11 months late is painful and expensive
- [ ] **W-9 and W-8BEN on file before first payment** — make this a rule in your contractor onboarding checklist; you cannot issue 1099s or 1042-Ss without them
- [ ] **Track all contractor payments $600+** with a running log — make 1099/1042-S season in January a non-event
- [ ] **Document any business-use mileage** if founders or contractors use personal vehicles for client meetings
- [ ] **Separate revenue recognition from cash** — if you sell annual contracts, revenue is recognized monthly (ASC 606). Your bookkeeping should reflect deferred revenue, which matters for accurate P&L and future due diligence

## Common Pitfalls

- **🚨 BOI filing not done.** FinCEN penalties are severe and widely misunderstood by founders. This is a 15-minute task — do not let it slip past day 1 of reading this plan.

- **💸 Ignoring the Brazil contractor withholding obligation.** No US-Brazil tax treaty means potential 30% withholding on US-source income. If you've been paying this contractor without withholding, you may have a back-liability. Your CPA needs to assess this immediately — voluntary disclosure is always cheaper than an IRS exam.

- **📊 Registering for sales tax in non-SaaS-taxable states.** The instinct when learning about economic nexus is to register everywhere. Don't. Registering creates an ongoing filing obligation even if you owe $0. Only register where your product is taxable AND you have nexus.

- **📋 Delaware franchise tax shock in March.** The Authorized Shares method default can produce a bill of $50K+ for a standard VC-backed cap table. Always calculate both methods and use APVC. Your registered agent's online portal will offer the choice — make sure you or your CPA selects APVC before hitting submit.

- **📁 No W-8BEN on file for foreign contractors.** If the IRS examines your Form 1042 compliance and you can't produce signed W-8BENs, every payment becomes potentially underwithheld and subject to penalties. Make this a contractor Day 1 requirement, retroactively collect any missing forms now.

- **🏢 Forgetting foreign qualification when you hire your first employee.** The moment you put a W-2 employee on payroll in a new state, that state almost certainly considers you to be "doing business" there — triggering foreign qualification, state income tax nexus, withholding accounts, SUI, and possibly workers' comp. Have a checklist ready to execute the day you make that hire.
