## State Framework: California AB5 (ABC Test)

**Framework:** California ABC Test under **AB5**, codified at **Labor Code §2775**.

A worker in California is **presumed an employee** unless the hiring entity can satisfy **all three prongs**. Failing even one → mandatory W-2.

| Prong | Question | Facts Here | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Is the worker free from the company's control and direction in performing the work? | Fixed M-F 9–5 schedule, 40 hrs/wk. Company dictates when and how work happens. | ❌ **FAILS** |
| **B** | Does the worker perform work *outside* the usual course of the company's business? | This is a software startup. The lead software engineer *is* the product. | ❌ **FAILS** |
| **C** | Is the worker customarily engaged in an independently established trade or business of the same nature? | Zero other clients. No independent business. | ❌ **FAILS** |

**AB5 verdict: Fails A, B, and C → W-2 employee. Full stop. The federal analysis below confirms this, but AB5 alone is dispositive.**

## IRS Three-Category Analysis

### Behavioral Control
**Lean: Strong W-2**

- **Type/degree of instructions:** Worker follows a fixed Monday–Friday, 9–5 schedule set by the company — direct scheduling control.
- **Training/equipment:** Company-issued MacBook signals the company controls the tools and work environment.
- **Evaluation:** Full-time engagement at a single company implies process-level oversight, not just outcome review.

### Financial Control
**Lean: Strong W-2**

- **Method of payment:** Hourly at $150/hr is the paradigmatic W-2 payment structure — leans W-2 per the *method of payment* factor. A flat-fee-per-deliverable arrangement would point toward 1099.
- **Worker's investment in equipment:** Zero — company provides the MacBook. No independent capital investment by the worker.
- **Worker's services available to the broader market:** Worker has *no other clients*, meaning they are entirely economically dependent on this company.
- **Profit or loss:** An hourly worker at a fixed rate cannot realize profit or loss through entrepreneurial judgment.

### Relationship of the Parties
**Lean: Strong W-2**

- **Permanency:** Open-ended, full-time engagement — not project-bounded.
- **Services integral to the business:** A lead software engineer at a software startup is not ancillary — they *are* the company's core product/service. This is one of the heaviest W-2 signals in the entire analysis.
- **Benefits:** No benefits are offered, which is a neutral-to-slight 1099 signal — but it is trivially outweighed by every other factor here, and absent benefits do **not** override the California ABC test.

## Cost of Misclassification

If you proceed with 1099 anyway and are audited:

- **Back payroll taxes:** 7.65% employer FICA share × all wages paid × years of lookback (IRS can go back 3 years on returns, unlimited on fraud). At $150/hr × 40 hrs/wk × 52 weeks = **~$312,000/year** in wages → ~$23,900/year in employer FICA alone, before penalties and interest.
- **California penalties:** Up to **$25,000 per misclassified worker** under Labor Code §226.8, plus potential criminal liability for willful misclassification. The EDD runs its own audit program independently of the IRS.
- **Worker claims:** The worker can sue retroactively for unpaid overtime (CA requires 1.5× after 8 hrs/day, not just 40 hrs/wk), denied meal/rest breaks, unreimbursed expenses, and any benefits a similarly-situated employee would have received. California has a **4-year statute of limitations** for UCL claims.
- **IRS Section 3509 relief** (reduced rates for unintentional misclassification) may be available — but requires the worker was consistently treated as 1099 and all 1099-NECs were filed. It does not eliminate the liability; it reduces it.

## Common Founder Mistakes — Specific to This Situation

- **"We have an IC agreement so we're safe."** An Independent Contractor Agreement is not a classification. California courts and the EDD look at the *actual working relationship*, not the contract label. A signed IC agreement with a W-2 worker makes the misclassification willful — which increases penalties.
- **"They're okay with being 1099 — they even prefer it."** The worker's preference is legally irrelevant. Classification is determined by the economic reality of the relationship, not what either party calls it or wants.
- **"$150/hr sounds like a contractor rate."** Hourly rate level is irrelevant to classification. Hourly *payment structure* actually leans W-2 under the IRS financial-control test. High pay does not equal contractor status.
- **"We're a small startup, the IRS won't come after us."** The EDD (California's employment agency) aggressively audits startups, especially in tech. A single disgruntled worker filing for unemployment benefits — which a 1099 worker technically can't receive — routinely triggers an EDD audit of the entire company's payroll practices.
- **"We'll fix it later when we have more resources."** Back taxes and penalties accrue from day one. The longer the misclassification continues, the larger the retroactive liability. Payroll setup in Gusto takes about 30 minutes — far cheaper than the audit.
