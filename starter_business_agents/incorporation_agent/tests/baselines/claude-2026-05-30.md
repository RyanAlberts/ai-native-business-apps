## Recommendation Summary

Form a **single-member LLC in Texas**. It gives you personal liability protection, costs $300 to file, has essentially zero annual maintenance cost at your revenue level, and is taxed as a pass-through — meaning no corporate-level tax and no separate business return until you elect otherwise. There is no compelling reason to form in Delaware (that's a VC-track play), and no reason to elect S-Corp status until your net profit is high enough that the payroll-tax savings outweigh the added compliance cost — a conversation to have with your CPA once you're consistently clearing $50–60K in profit.

## Entity Type

**Single-Member LLC**

For a solo, bootstrapped SaaS founder with no employees and no outside investors, an LLC is the simplest structure that still draws a clean line between your personal assets and the business. A C-Corp would add unnecessary complexity (board formalities, potential double taxation, Delaware franchise tax) with no offsetting benefit given your bootstrapped, no-VC plans. A sole proprietorship costs nothing to form but exposes your personal assets — not appropriate once you're selling software to accounting firms who could sue over errors or data issues.

**Tax treatment:** By default, the IRS treats a single-member LLC as a "disregarded entity" — profits and losses flow directly to your personal Schedule C. Discuss with your CPA whether a future S-Corp election (Form 2553) makes sense once your SaaS profit is substantial; it can reduce self-employment tax but requires running payroll for yourself, which adds cost and complexity.

## State of Formation

**Texas**

You live here, your business will operate here, and Texas is genuinely one of the best states for a bootstrapped founder. There is no state income tax — what you earn flows to your federal return with no Texas income tax bite. The one-time filing fee is **$300** (Certificate of Formation). The ongoing annual cost is effectively **$0** for most small businesses: Texas has no annual report filing; you file a franchise tax return each May 15, but the "No Tax Due" threshold is $2.47M in annualized revenue — you will almost certainly owe $0 and just check a box for years. Forming in Wyoming or Nevada instead would still require you to register as a foreign LLC in Texas (adding cost and complexity) since you operate here. Delaware would do the same, plus add Delaware franchise tax. Stay home.

**Filing fee:** $300 (Certificate of Formation, per the Texas SoS)
**Ongoing cost:** ~$0/year below the $2.47M franchise tax threshold (franchise tax filing still required annually)

## Filing Checklist

1. **Verify business name availability** at the [Texas SOSDirect name search](https://direct.sos.state.tx.us/acct/acct-login.asp) (requires a free SOSDirect account). Search your exact proposed name *and* the root keyword — Texas reserves names that are "deceptively similar," not just identical. *(You didn't provide a name — once you have one, ask me to run a `state_business_name_search` for it so I can surface the direct search link.)*

2. **File your Certificate of Formation** (Texas's equivalent of Articles of Organization) with the [Texas Secretary of State](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/forms_boc.shtml). Fee: **$300**. You can file online via SOSDirect or mail Form 205. Processing is typically 3–5 business days online.

3. **Designate a Registered Agent** — required at the time of filing. See the *Registered Agent Options* section below. Review the [Texas SoS registered agent guidelines](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/registeredagent.shtml) for requirements (must be a Texas physical address, not a PO box).

4. **Apply for an EIN** at the [IRS EIN online portal](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) — free and takes ~15 minutes. Do this immediately after your Certificate of Formation is approved. You'll need the EIN before opening a business bank account.

5. **Confirm your BOI status.** Under FinCEN's interim final rule effective March 26, 2025, **US-formed entities are exempt** from the Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report — only foreign companies registered to do business in the US must file. As a Texas-formed LLC, no BOI filing is required. Verify current status at the [FinCEN BOI FAQ](https://www.fincen.gov/boi-faqs) — this rule has changed multiple times and you should confirm it hasn't changed again before relying on the exemption.

6. **Draft a Single-Member Operating Agreement.** Texas does not require you to file one, but every lender, bank, and enterprise customer you'll encounter will ask for it. It also reinforces the liability shield. See template options in *Estimated Costs* below.

7. **Open a dedicated business bank account** using your EIN and Certificate of Formation. Never commingle personal and business funds — this is the #1 way founders accidentally pierce the LLC veil.

8. **Register for Texas franchise tax** through the [Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/). You'll receive a Taxpayer Number when you form. Even if you owe $0, the annual No Tax Due report is due **May 15** each year.

9. **Check city/county business license requirements.** Texas has no statewide business license, but many cities (Austin, Dallas, Houston, etc.) require a local general business license or "Certificate of Occupancy" for home-based businesses. Contact your city's business office — fees typically run $0–$75.

10. **Consider a trademark search** if your SaaS brand name is important. Search existing marks at [USPTO TESS](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/search/search-information) before spending money on branding.

## Registered Agent Options

### 1. Act as Your Own Registered Agent

**Free**, but your name and Texas physical address (not a PO box — Texas requires a street address) become permanent public record in the SoS database. If you work from home, that means your home address is searchable forever. You must also be physically present at that address during normal business hours to accept service of process — if you're at a conference or on vacation when a lawsuit is served, you can miss critical legal deadlines. Re-filing is required if your address ever changes. **Best for:** founders with a dedicated commercial office address and no privacy concerns.

### 2. Traditional Registered Agent Services

- **Northwest Registered Agent** — ~$125/year. Consistently the top pick among bootstrapped founders: strong privacy practices (uses their address on all public filings, not yours), fast mail scanning, no data selling, US-based human phone support, and zero upsell pressure. Hard to beat for the price.
- **Registered Agents Inc.** — ~$200/year. Premium service with excellent multi-state coverage. Overkill for a solo Texas operation today, but worth considering if you later register in multiple states.
- **Harbor Compliance** — ~$99–$200/year. Strong compliance tooling and reminders; slightly bureaucratic dashboard. Good fit for compliance-sensitive industries, though a SaaS for accountants isn't unusually regulated from a registered-agent standpoint.
- **LegalZoom** — ~$249/year. Strong brand recognition but notorious for upselling operating agreement packages, compliance add-ons, and annual report services you can handle yourself. Mixed customer service reviews. Only choose this if you already have a LegalZoom subscription.

### 3. Digital / Online-First RA Services

- **Bizee (formerly IncFile)** — Free RA for year 1, ~$119/year thereafter. Cheapest entry point; service quality has improved but customer support remains inconsistent.
- **ZenBusiness** — ~$199/year. Clean dashboard and good UX; some upsell pressure toward their compliance packages.
- **Doola** — ~$300/year, bundled with formation. Targets digital-first and non-US founders; fast mail scanning and app-based access. Likely overkill for a Texas-based founder forming locally.
- **Stripe Atlas** — RA included in their $500 formation bundle, but **Delaware C-Corps only** — not applicable here.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Commingling personal and business finances.** The #1 way to accidentally lose your liability protection ("pierce the veil") is running business expenses through your personal account or transferring money without documentation. Open a separate business checking account the week your EIN arrives — before your first invoice goes out.

- **Skipping the Operating Agreement because Texas doesn't require filing it.** Banks, enterprise accounting firms, and future payment processors (Stripe, Braintree, etc.) will ask for it. More importantly, it's your primary evidence that the LLC is a real, separate business — not just a name you added to your personal account. Do this in month one.

- **Wrong S-Corp election timing.** If your CPA recommends electing S-Corp status, the IRS Form 2553 must be filed within 75 days of formation (or by March 15 of the tax year you want it to apply). Miss the window and you wait a full calendar year. Don't let this decision drift — put a calendar reminder to discuss it with your CPA within 60 days of formation.

- **Assuming you're exempt from the Texas franchise tax filing entirely.** You almost certainly owe $0 — but you still must file the No Tax Due report by May 15 every year. Failing to file, even when you owe nothing, puts your LLC in "not in good standing" status, which can prevent you from renewing contracts with enterprise clients or opening new bank accounts.

- **Underestimating liability exposure when selling to accounting firms.** Your customers will handle sensitive client financial data inside your SaaS. A poorly drafted (or missing) Terms of Service and Data Processing Agreement can expose you to claims far exceeding your SaaS revenue. Before you sign your first paid contract, have an attorney review your ToS — or at minimum use a professionally drafted SaaS agreement template designed for B2B software. This is the one place where the $300–$1,500 attorney cost is genuinely worth it.
