## Recommendation Summary

Form a **Texas single-member LLC**, registered in Texas. It gives you the liability shield you need, keeps your taxes simple (pass-through to your personal return), costs a flat $300 to file, and Texas's lack of state income tax means more of your SaaS revenue stays in your pocket. ## Entity Type

**Single-Member LLC**

A single-member LLC walls off your personal assets (home, savings, personal accounts) from any business liability — critical for a SaaS product where a data breach, contract dispute, or IP claim could otherwise reach your personal finances. It's far simpler to maintain than a corporation (no board minutes, no stock issuance), which is right-sized for a year-1 bootstrapped solo founder.

**Tax treatment:** By default, the IRS treats a single-member LLC as a "disregarded entity" — profit and loss flow to your Schedule C. When your net income grows consistently (often cited at $50K–$80K+ net), discuss with your CPA whether an **S-Corp election (Form 2553)** makes sense to reduce self-employment tax via a salary/distribution split. Don't elect it now — the added payroll administration isn't worth it until revenue justifies it.

## State of Formation

**Texas**

Since you have no plans to raise venture capital, Delaware offers you no meaningful advantage — it exists primarily for institutional investors who want Delaware's predictable case law and preferred stock mechanics. Forming in Texas instead means:

- **No state income tax** — your LLC's pass-through income isn't taxed at the state level.
- **No annual report** — Texas replaced it with a franchise tax filing, and most small businesses owe **$0** under the No Tax Due threshold (currently $2.47M in annualized revenue). You still must *file* by May 15 each year, but the cost is typically nothing.
- **No foreign qualification** — if you formed in Delaware but operate in Texas, you'd pay Delaware's ~$300 formation fee *plus* Texas's $750 foreign-registration fee, plus registered agents in both states. That's a headache you don't need.
- **Filing fee:** $300 (Certificate of Formation, from tool)
- **Annual maintenance:** ~$0 franchise tax for most small SaaS entities under the $2.47M threshold; filing is still required.

## Filing Checklist

1. **Verify your proposed business name** at the [Texas SOS SOSDirect name search](https://direct.sos.state.tx.us/acct/acct-login.asp). You'll need to create a free SOSDirect account. Search your exact name *and* the root word — if you plan to trademark it, also run a parallel search at the [USPTO trademark database (TESS)](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/search/search-information).

2. **File your Certificate of Formation** (Texas's term for Articles of Organization) with the [Texas Secretary of State](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/corp/forms_boc.shtml). Fee: **$300**, paid to the SOS. You can file online via SOSDirect or mail Form 205. Processing time is typically 3–5 business days online.

3. **Designate a registered agent** at the time of filing — see *Registered Agent Options* below. The agent's Texas physical address goes on your Certificate of Formation.

4. **Apply for an EIN** at the [IRS EIN online portal](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) — free, ~15 minutes, instant issuance. Do this the same day your Certificate of Formation is approved. You need the EIN before opening a business bank account.

5. **Open a dedicated business bank account** immediately after receiving your EIN. Commingling personal and business funds is the #1 way solo LLC owners accidentally pierce their own liability shield.

6. **Draft and sign an Operating Agreement.** Texas does not require you to file it, but you should have one on record. For a single-member LLC it's brief — it records your ownership, management authority, and how you'd handle dissolution. See options in Estimated Costs below.

7. **Check BOI/FinCEN status.** Under FinCEN's interim final rule effective March 26, 2025, entities formed in the US (like your Texas LLC) are **exempt** from the Beneficial Ownership Information report — only foreign companies registering to do business in the US must file. Verify current status at the [FinCEN BOI FAQ](https://www.fincen.gov/boi-faqs) before assuming the rule hasn't changed again.

8. **Register for Texas franchise tax** at the [Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/). Your first report is due May 15 of the year after formation. At your revenue level, you'll almost certainly owe $0 — but you still must file.

9. **Obtain any required local business licenses.** Texas has no state general business license, but your city or county may require one. Check your municipality's website; typical cost is $25–$75/year.

10. **Trademark search (optional but recommended).** If your SaaS name or logo is core to your brand, run a full search on [USPTO TESS](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/search/search-information) and consider filing a 1(b) intent-to-use application. This is separate from state name registration.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Commingling personal and business finances.** As a single-member LLC, a court can "pierce the corporate veil" and hold you personally liable if you routinely pay personal expenses from the business account or vice versa. Open the business account the day your EIN arrives and never mix funds.

- **Skipping the franchise tax filing because you owe nothing.** Texas still requires the annual franchise tax *report* even if your tax due is $0 under the No Tax Due threshold. Failing to file two years in a row triggers forfeiture of your LLC's right to operate — you'd have to pay back fees and penalties to reinstate.

- **Premature S-Corp election.** It's tempting to elect S-Corp status right away, but before you have predictable revenue and can pay yourself a "reasonable salary," the election creates payroll complexity (quarterly payroll tax deposits, W-2, potential state unemployment insurance) that outweighs the SE-tax savings. Discuss timing with your CPA once net income consistently exceeds ~$50–80K/year.

- **Assuming your SaaS is exempt from sales tax everywhere.** Texas imposes sales tax on certain data processing and software-as-a-service revenue. Depending on your product and how it's delivered, you may have Texas sales tax obligations from day one. Talk to a Texas CPA about this before you invoice your first customer — retroactive sales tax exposure is painful.

- **Not protecting your business name at the federal level.** State name registration with the Texas SOS only prevents another Texas LLC from using the same name *in Texas*. It gives you no protection against a competitor in another state or a prior federal trademark holder. If your SaaS brand matters, budget for a USPTO trademark search and application early.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Missing the 83(b) election window.** You have exactly **30 calendar days** from the date of your restricted stock purchase to file with the IRS. Both founders must file separately. Your vesting schedule means that without an 83(b), you'll owe ordinary income tax on the fair market value of shares as they vest — potentially a massive bill right as you're raising your seed round. Set a calendar alarm the day you sign your stock purchase agreements.

- **Delaware franchise tax shock on the authorized-shares method.** If your attorney files with, say, 10,000,000 authorized shares at $0.0001 par value and you don't request the assumed par value calculation, Delaware's default formula can produce a franchise tax bill of $85,000+. The assumed par value method typically brings this down to ~$400–$500 for an early-stage startup. Confirm with your attorney at filing time which method will be used.

- **Assuming your seed-stage fintech doesn't need a money transmitter license.** Depending on your product (payments, lending, stored value, remittance), you may need state money transmitter licenses before you launch — not just before you raise. California's Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) is aggressive. Get a fintech-specific attorney to review your product flow early.

- **Issuing stock options before your 409A valuation is complete.** Options issued without a compliant 409A valuation are subject to IRC §409A penalties — a 20% excise tax plus interest on the employee, not the company. You must have the 409A in place before you grant your first option to any engineer. Budget 3–4 weeks for the valuation process.

- **Forgetting California's foreign-qualification clock.** California law requires you to register as a foreign corporation "before entering into repeated and successive transactions of its business" in California — not just after you're profitable or have customers. If you're building, hiring, and operating in California, you are already subject to this requirement. Delaying the foreign qualification to save the $800 franchise tax is a gamble that CA FTB will notice, and the penalties for back taxes + interest will far exceed any savings.

4. **Apply for an EIN** (Employer Identification Number) at the [IRS EIN Online Portal](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). Free, ~15 minutes. Do this immediately after formation — you need it to open a business bank account and for supplier/platform relationships.

5. **Confirm your FinCEN BOI status.** Under FinCEN's interim final rule (effective March 26, 2025), companies formed **in the US** are **exempt** from the Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report — only foreign entities registered to do business in the US must file. Your Wyoming LLC is US-formed, so **no BOI filing is required**. Confirm current status at the [FinCEN BOI FAQ](https://www.fincen.gov/boi-faqs) — this area of law has changed repeatedly and you should verify before assuming permanent exemption.

6. **Draft and sign an Operating Agreement.** Wyoming doesn't legally require one, but without it, state default LLC rules govern disputes and courts treat your LLC less seriously. For a single-member LLC this is a straightforward document.

7. **Open a dedicated business bank account.** This is non-negotiable for preserving your liability shield. Never commingle personal and business funds.

8. **Register with Wyoming's Department of Revenue** if you have sales-tax nexus. Wyoming has a 4% state sales tax; drop-shippers may trigger nexus obligations — discuss with your CPA, especially given marketplace facilitator rules on platforms like Amazon or Shopify.

9. **Obtain any required business licenses.** Wyoming has no general state business license, but check your county/municipality for local requirements at [Wyoming SoS Business Filings](https://sos.wyo.gov/Business/Default.aspx).

10. **File your annual report** each year at [Wyoming Annual Report Portal](https://sos.wyo.gov/Business/AnnualReport.aspx). Due by the first day of your anniversary month. Fee: ~$60 minimum.

## Registered Agent Options

### 1. Act as Your Own Registered Agent — $0

You can list yourself, but your **personal Wyoming address becomes permanent public record**, searchable by anyone. For a home-based ecommerce founder this creates junk mail, solicitation, and doxxing risks. You must also be physically present at that address during business hours on any business day — you can't be at a supplier meeting or on vacation when a lawsuit is served. Any address change requires re-filing with the state ($0 fee in Wyoming, but it's friction). **Best for:** founders with a separate commercial street address and zero privacy concerns.

### 2. Traditional Registered Agent Services — $100–$250/year

- **Northwest Registered Agent** — ~$125/year. The consensus founder favorite. Strong privacy practices (uses their address on your public filings, not yours), scans all mail and uploads it digitally, doesn't sell your data, US-based phone support. Excellent value.
- **Registered Agents Inc.** — ~$200/year. Premium tier with strong multi-state support. Overkill for a single-state Wyoming LLC today, but worth knowing if you expand.
- **Harbor Compliance** — ~$99–$200/year. Solid compliance-focused service, slightly more bureaucratic interface. Better suited to regulated industries.
- **LegalZoom** — ~$249/year. Strong brand recognition but aggressive upsells on compliance packages, operating agreement add-ons, etc. Only worth it if you're already in a LegalZoom bundle.

### 3. Digital / Online-First Registered Agent Services — $0–$300/year

- **Bizee (formerly IncFile)** — Free RA for year 1, ~$119/year thereafter. Cheapest entry point; service reliability has improved but customer reviews are inconsistent.
- **ZenBusiness** — ~$199/year. Clean dashboard, decent UX; some upsell pressure.
- **Doola** — ~$300/year (typically bundled with formation). Strong for digital-first and non-US founders; fast mail scanning.
- **Stripe Atlas** — Included in $500 formation package, but only for Delaware C-Corps. Not relevant here.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Piercing the corporate veil through commingled finances.** Drop-shippers in particular are tempted to use a single card for personal and business purchases. The moment a plaintiff's attorney can show you treated the LLC as your personal piggy bank, your liability shield evaporates. Open the business bank account before your first sale.

- **Assuming your supplier's insurance covers you.** It doesn't — not fully, and not reliably. As a drop-shipper you are in the distribution chain. Consider a separate **commercial general liability (CGL) or product-liability rider** on top of the LLC shield; the LLC limits personal exposure but doesn't pay for your defense costs.

- **Getting caught by multi-state sales-tax nexus.** Wyoming has a 4% sales tax and economic nexus rules (~$100K or 200 transactions). If you're selling on Shopify or Amazon, marketplace facilitator laws may shift the collection obligation to the platform — but you still need to understand your exposure in other states. Confirm with your CPA before your first full year of revenue.

- **Missing the Wyoming annual report deadline.** Wyoming annual reports are due the first day of your formation anniversary month — not a calendar-year December deadline. Missing it triggers penalties and eventually administrative dissolution, which voids your liability protection retroactively. Set a calendar reminder the day you form.

- **Waiting too long to consider an S-Corp election.** Form 2553 has strict timing rules — generally within 75 days of formation or by March 15 of the prior tax year. If you hit $60K+ in net profit and want the self-employment tax savings, you need to act on the election before the window closes. Flag this for your CPA at your first tax meeting.
