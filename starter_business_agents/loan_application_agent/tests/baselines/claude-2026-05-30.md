## Matched programs (ranked)

---

**1. SBA 7(a) Loan — Equipment**
- **Loan amount range:** $50,000–$5,000,000 (your $120k is squarely in the sweet spot)
- **Typical rate/fee:** Variable or fixed; currently ranging approximately Prime + 2.25–2.75% for loans of this size (current rates vary — confirm with your lender). SBA guarantee fee of ~2–3% on the guaranteed portion applies; some lenders roll this into the loan.
- **Eligibility highlights:** U.S.-based for-profit business ✓, under SBA size standard for bakeries ✓, 2+ years in operation ✓, owner has good credit ✓, use of funds is eligible (equipment) ✓
- **Approximate timeline:** 30–90 days from complete application to funding; faster (~21 days) through SBA Preferred Lenders (PLP)
- **Why this for this founder:** Flexible terms (up to 10 years on equipment), competitive rate, and your home equity satisfies the collateral requirement most lenders require.

**Fit: Strong** — Two years of profitable operations, growth trajectory, and real collateral remove the biggest friction points for 7(a) approval.

---

**2. Conventional Community Bank Equipment Loan**
- **Loan amount range:** Typically $25,000–$500,000
- **Typical rate/fee:** Approximately 7–11% fixed for creditworthy borrowers (current rates vary). Fewer fees than SBA.
- **Eligibility highlights:** At 720 credit, $280k revenue, and zero existing business debt, many community banks and credit unions in the Treasure Valley will underwrite this without SBA backing.
- **Approximate timeline:** 2–4 weeks if relationship is already established; 4–6 weeks for a new lender
- **Why this for this founder:** Simpler paperwork, no SBA guarantee fee, and faster close — worth asking your existing bank *first* before going the SBA route.

**Fit: Strong** — Clean profile makes this a real option; the only risk is if the lender wants more than 2 years of business history or a larger equity injection.

---

**3. Idaho Commerce Collateral Support Program**
- **Loan amount range:** Supports loans up to $2,000,000 (pledges cash collateral to fill gaps)
- **Typical rate/fee:** No direct cost to borrower; the program charges a small fee to the lender. Works *alongside* a bank loan, not as a standalone.
- **Eligibility highlights:** Idaho-based business ✓, job creation or retention component (expanding a bakery likely qualifies) ✓, used when borrower has a collateral gap
- **Approximate timeline:** Administered through participating Idaho lenders; adds 1–2 weeks to a conventional loan close
- **Why this for this founder:** If a local bank wants more collateral than your home equity alone, this state program fills the gap without requiring you to tap personal savings.

**Fit: Moderate** — You may not need this given your home equity, but it's a valuable backstop if a lender says "you're $20–30k short on collateral."

---

**4. SBA 504 Loan**
- **Loan amount range:** Minimum ~$125,000 (project size); split structure: ~50% bank / ~40% SBA debenture / ~10% borrower injection
- **Typical rate/fee:** Bank portion at market rate; SBA debenture at a fixed rate (currently in the 6–7% range, varies). Higher closing costs.
- **Eligibility highlights:** Equipment must have a useful life of 10+ years ✓ (most commercial bakery equipment qualifies). However, the 10% equity injection means $12k out of pocket, and the two-lender structure adds complexity.
- **Approximate timeline:** 60–120 days
- **Why this for this founder:** The fixed long-term rate on the SBA portion is attractive if rates stay elevated — but the structure is overkill for a $120k ask.

**Fit: Moderate** — Technically eligible, but the added complexity and longer timeline make 7(a) a better first choice at this loan size. Revisit 504 if you're ever buying real estate or doing a $300k+ equipment package.

**5. CDFI Loan (e.g., Opportunity Resources or Idaho-area CDFI)**
- **Loan amount range:** $5,000–$250,000 depending on CDFI
- **Typical rate/fee:** Often 6–10%, sometimes below-market for qualifying businesses; fees vary
- **Eligibility highlights:** CDFIs often target underserved borrowers or early-stage businesses. Your profile (720 credit, $280k revenue) likely over-qualifies, but worth knowing if banks say no.
- **Approximate timeline:** 30–60 days
- **Why this for this founder:** A fallback if conventional and SBA paths stall; also useful for bridging while a larger loan processes.

**Fit: Weak** — Your strong profile is likely better served by bank-track or SBA financing at more favorable terms. Keep this in the back pocket.

## Application package outline

1. **Loan application form** — Lender's standard commercial loan application (or SBA Form 1919 for 7(a))
2. **Personal financial statement** — SBA Form 413 (or lender equivalent); lists all personal assets/liabilities including home equity
3. **Business tax returns — 2 years** — Full 1065/1120S/Schedule C as applicable, with all schedules
4. **Personal tax returns — 2–3 years** — Owner's 1040s, all schedules
5. **YTD Profit & Loss statement** — Prepared by your bookkeeper or accountant; dated within 90 days of application
6. **YTD Balance sheet** — Same date as P&L
7. **3 months business bank statements** — All pages, all accounts
8. **Entity documents** — Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation letter, Operating Agreement
9. **Equipment vendor quotes** — 2–3 written quotes for the specific equipment, showing model/specs and price
10. **Use-of-funds memo** — 1-page breakdown: what equipment, what it costs, and how it increases production/revenue
11. **Home equity documentation** — Current mortgage statement + property value evidence (county assessment or recent appraisal)
12. **Brief business narrative** — 1–2 pages: what the bakery does, customers served, why this equipment, growth plan. Attach any wholesale contracts, catering agreements, or letters of intent if you have them.

## Common pitfalls (specific to this founder)

- **Under-documenting the revenue growth story.** A +30% YoY trend is your biggest asset — but only if the lender can *see* it. Make sure your P&L and bank statements clearly show the trajectory. A one-paragraph narrative explaining *why* you're growing (new wholesale account, catering contracts, location foot traffic) makes underwriters confident it's sustainable.
- **Mixing personal and business finances.** At $280k revenue, commingled transactions are a red flag. Before applying, make sure every business expense has hit the business account — not your personal card — for at least the last 3 months of statements the lender will review.
- **Pledging home equity without understanding the lien.** Your $120k in equity is a clean collateral match for a $120k loan — but a lender taking a second position on your home is a real obligation. Make sure you understand the terms and have a realistic repayment plan before signing.
- **Getting vendor quotes that expire.** Equipment quotes typically have a 30–60 day validity window. If your loan takes 60–90 days (common for SBA 7(a)), your quotes may expire before closing. Get quotes dated as late as possible and ask vendors upfront about reissuance.
- **Applying at peak season for SBA lenders.** SBA lender pipelines often back up at fiscal year-end (September) and post-holiday (January–February). You're applying in a relatively clear window now — move promptly so you don't drift into a backlog period.
