## Common pitfalls (specific to this founder)

- **Under-borrowing and returning in 6 months.** Commercial kitchen installations routinely exceed quotes by 10–20% (electrical upgrades, delivery, installation, permit fees). Request a 10% contingency in your loan amount upfront — lenders expect it on equipment deals.

- **Pledging your home without fully understanding the terms.** Home equity as collateral means a payment default could put your residence at risk. Before signing, make sure you understand exactly what triggers default, whether there's a cure period, and how the lender would pursue collateral. The personal guarantee is real; read the note carefully.

- **Making multiple simultaneous applications without a strategy.** Each hard credit pull can nudge your score down; worse, some SBA lenders will decline an application if they see a recent denial elsewhere. Work your top path first, wait for a decision, then go to Plan B only if necessary.

- **NAICS code mismatch.** Retail bakeries should file under NAICS **311811** (Retail Bakeries) or **311812** (Commercial Bakeries). If your EIN was registered under a café, restaurant, or generic food service code, the SBA size standard check may flag it. Verify your NAICS code on your IRS and state filings matches what you tell the lender before submitting.

- **Disorganized books killing your rate or approval.** If your bookkeeping is on a 3-month lag or informally maintained, pay a bookkeeper to clean up and reconcile your records *before* applying. Lenders who see messy financials price in more risk — or pass entirely. Two months of clean, CPA-reviewed books is worth the $500–800 it costs to produce professionally.
