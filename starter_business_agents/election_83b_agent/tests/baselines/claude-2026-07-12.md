## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **No certified-mail return receipt.** Regular first-class mail gives you no proof of the postmark date. If the IRS claims non-receipt or late filing, you have no defense. Always use USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt (green card) or the IRS e-file portal.
- **Mailing to the wrong IRS service center.** Texas filers go to Ogden, UT — not Austin or any other center. Sending to the wrong center may be treated as if no election was filed. Verify at the IRS URL above before you postmark.
- **Filing "one election for the company."** The §83(b) election is made *per taxpayer*, not per company. If Acme Books later issues restricted stock to employees, advisors, or co-founders, **each person must file their own separate election** within 30 days of their own grant date.
- **Forgetting to send a copy to the issuer.** Treasury Reg. §1.83-2(d) requires the taxpayer to furnish a copy of the election to the person for whom services are performed. File a copy with Acme Books Inc. on the same day you mail to the IRS.
- **Attaching the election to your tax return.** This used to be required but was eliminated by TD 9779 (effective 2016). Attaching it now wastes paper but isn't harmful; *not filing with the IRS within 30 days* is fatal and cannot be undone.
- **Missing the 30-day window and assuming late relief is easy.** The 30-calendar-day deadline is a statutory bright line. The IRS grants §9100 relief for missed §83(b) elections only in narrow circumstances, and it requires a private letter ruling. Do not count on it. Mail by **2026-05-31**.
- **Reporting an FMV above the price paid without a 409A.** Jane's situation is clean ($0.0001 FMV = $0.0001 price paid), but founders who report a spread (FMV > price paid) at founding without a 409A valuation to support it risk IRS scrutiny. Jane has no spread here — no issue.

## Common Mistakes

- **Mailing without Certified Mail Return Receipt.** Without the USPS green card (PS Form 3811), you have no timestamped proof of postmark. The IRS can treat the filing as never received. Always get the receipt and keep it forever.

- **Sending both founders' elections in one envelope or on one letter.** The §83(b) election is per taxpayer. Two founders = two letters = two separate envelopes with two certified-mail receipts. A single combined letter is not a valid election for either person.

- **Mailing to the wrong IRS service center.** California filers go to Ogden, UT. An election routed to the wrong center may be treated as not timely received. Verify at the IRS lookup URL before you drop it in the mail.

- **Missing the 30-day postmark deadline.** There is no grace period. The deadline is a statutory bright line (§83(b)(2)). IRS authority to grant extensions (§9100 relief) is exceedingly narrow and is rarely granted for routine 83(b) elections. Do not rely on it.

- **Forgetting to send a copy to the issuer.** Treasury Regulation §1.83-2(d) requires the taxpayer to furnish a copy of the election to the company on or before the date the election is filed. This is mandatory, not courtesy.

- **Leaving the TIN blank or typing it into a digital file.** Fill in your SSN/ITIN by hand in ink on the printed letter. Never type your full SSN into an email, shared document, or electronic form that isn't a secured government portal.
