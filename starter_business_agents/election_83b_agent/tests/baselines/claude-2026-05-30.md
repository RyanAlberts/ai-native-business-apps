## Tax Savings Illustration

Using Jane's actual numbers:

| Scenario | Tax event | Amount of ordinary income recognized |
|---|---|---|
| **With §83(b) filed** | At grant (May 1, 2026) | FMV − Price Paid = $800 − $800 = **$0** |
| **Without §83(b) filed** | At each monthly vesting tranche over 4 years | Ordinary income = (FMV at that vesting date − $0.0001) × shares vesting |

**Because FMV equals price paid ($0.0001/share), filing the §83(b) is the maximum-win scenario:**

- **$0 of ordinary income is recognized today.**
- The entire holding period starts May 1, 2026 for all 8,000,000 shares.
- When Jane sells (e.g., at an M&A exit or IPO), all appreciation above $800 will be taxed as **long-term capital gains** (assuming she holds >1 year from grant), currently taxed at 0–20%, *not* as ordinary income (currently up to 37%).
- Without the election, each monthly vesting event would be a taxable ordinary-income event at the FMV on that date — potentially very expensive if the company has grown by then, and administratively complex.

> **Example:** If the company is worth $1.00/share at the Series A (month 13, when cliff vests), without an 83(b) Jane would recognize ~$833,267 of ordinary income on that single tranche alone. With the §83(b) already filed, she recognizes $0 — she already "locked in" her $800 basis.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Mailing without Certified Mail + Return Receipt** → You have no proof of the postmark date. If the IRS claims the election was late, you cannot defend yourself. Always use Certified Mail.
- **Sending to the wrong IRS service center** → The IRS may treat the election as not filed. Verify the Ogden, UT address at the URL above before mailing.
- **Missing the issuer-copy requirement** → Treas. Reg. §1.83-2(d) requires you to give a copy to the company. Mail or hand-deliver it the same day.
- **Forgetting to fill in the TIN by hand** → The letter is incomplete without it. Write it in pen before signing; do not type it into a shared doc.
- **Assuming e-filing extends the deadline** → It doesn't. IRS Form 15620 (e-file) carries the exact same May 31, 2026 postmark deadline. The clock does not reset.
