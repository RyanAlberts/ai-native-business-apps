### Banking pitfalls

- **Commingling personal and business funds** is the #1 liability risk for a multi-member LLC. California courts look at this when evaluating whether to pierce the corporate veil. Day one, every business payment in and out goes through the business account.
- **Mercury's FDIC coverage is via a sweep network, not Mercury itself** — Mercury is not a bank. Your deposits are held at partner banks (e.g., Evolve, Choice). Confirm the current sweep partners and that your balance stays within the $250k-per-partner limits, or spread across the program's ~$5M aggregate coverage.
- **Missing the FinCEN Beneficial Ownership disclosure** is a federal compliance requirement (CTA 2024). Both founders must be disclosed. Failing to file the BOI report separately with FinCEN (not the bank) can trigger fines — this is separate from the bank's own collection of this info.
- **Using Venmo or personal PayPal to collect consulting fees** — even once — creates co-mingling evidence and often violates those platforms' business-use terms. Invoice through the business account from day one.

### Insurance pitfalls

### Policies this founder probably needs

#### 1. 🚛 Commercial Auto Insurance *(most urgent — get this before you drive the truck for business)*

- **Coverage:** $1M combined single limit liability; comprehensive + collision on the truck itself
- **Approximate annual cost:** $2,000–$4,500/yr (food trucks run higher than standard commercial vehicles due to propane/cooking equipment)
- **Why you need it:** Your personal auto policy explicitly excludes vehicles used commercially. The moment you drive that truck to a commissary or event, you are uninsured if you only have personal auto. One accident while driving to a gig = total out-of-pocket exposure.

#### 2. 🍔 General Liability + Product Liability *(bind before your first event)*

- **Coverage:** $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate; product liability sublimit of at least $1M
- **Approximate annual cost:** $700–$1,800/yr
- **Why you need it:** A customer slips near your service window, or 12 people report food poisoning after your event — GL + product liability covers both. Many Miami event venues and festivals *require* a $1M GL certificate naming them as additional insured before they'll let you set up.
- **Common deductible:** $500–$1,000
- **Note:** Confirm your policy explicitly includes **product liability** (some base GL policies exclude food-service product claims — read the exclusions).

---

#### 3. ⚙️ Inland Marine / Commercial Property + Equipment Breakdown *(before you invest in kitchen equipment)*

- **Coverage:** Equipment value — likely $15,000–$40,000 for a fitted food truck; equipment breakdown sublimit
- **Approximate annual cost:** $400–$900/yr
- **Why you need it:** Your commercial auto policy covers the truck *as a vehicle* — it typically does NOT cover the fryers, generators, refrigerators, POS terminals, and smallwares inside it. If your generator dies mid-event or your walk-in compressor fails, equipment breakdown coverage pays for repairs and may cover lost income.
- **Common deductible:** $500–$1,000

---

#### 4. 🧑‍🍳 Workers' Compensation *(before your part-time employee's first shift)*

- **Coverage:** Florida statutory limits (unlimited medical; wage replacement per schedule)
- **Approximate annual cost:** $800–$1,800/yr (food service classification code, Florida rates)
- **Why you need it:** Florida only *legally requires* workers' comp for non-construction businesses with **4 or more employees** — so you're technically exempt with one part-time worker. However, food service is physically demanding (burns, cuts, slip-and-falls are common), and if your employee is injured on the job without workers' comp, you bear 100% of their medical costs plus potential negligence claims. This is a $1,000 policy that protects you from a $50,000 problem.
- **Common deductible:** $0 (workers' comp rarely has a traditional deductible)
- **Florida note:** Even though it's not mandatory at your size, Florida's Division of Workers' Compensation can audit and issue stop-work orders. One part-time employee today can easily become four by summer festival season.

### Insurance pitfalls

- **Assuming commercial auto covers your kitchen equipment.** It covers the truck as a vehicle in motion. The $25,000 of equipment bolted inside is a separate coverage need — don't find this out after a theft or fire.
- **Skipping workers' comp because you're "under the threshold."** The legal threshold (4 employees) is not a safety threshold. One serious burn or fall and your part-time employee's ER visit, surgery, and missed wages come directly out of your pocket.
- **Not getting an Additional Insured endorsement before events.** Miami food truck festivals, markets, and corporate events will require it. Getting a policy that doesn't allow AI endorsements — or delays in issuing certificates — will cost you bookings.
- **Under-insuring the truck's equipment value.** Founders often insure at purchase price, not replacement cost. A commercial kitchen buildout that cost you $30K in 2023 may cost $40K+ to rebuild today. Insure at **replacement cost**, not actual cash value (ACV).
