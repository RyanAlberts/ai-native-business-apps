## Recommendation summary

For a two-person Delaware-formed, California-operating software consulting LLC serving healthcare clinics at $200k revenue, **Mercury** is the clear banking choice — digital-first, zero fees, deep accounting integrations, and FDIC sweep coverage that scales with your cash. On the insurance side, you need three policies now: **Tech E&O (Professional Liability)**, **Cyber Liability**, and **General Liability** — in that priority order. Healthcare clients will almost certainly require certificates of insurance before signing contracts, so don't treat these as optional.

### Documents to gather

- [ ] **EIN Confirmation Letter** (IRS CP 575 or 147C) — both founders will need this
- [ ] **Delaware Certificate of Formation** (your formation document from Delaware SOS)
- [ ] **LLC Operating Agreement** — virtually every bank requires this for multi-member LLCs; have it signed by both members
- [ ] **California Certificate of Authority** — you're a foreign LLC operating in California; Mercury and others will want evidence of CA registration
- [ ] **Government-issued photo ID** for both beneficial owners (each person owning ≥25%, or controlling members)
- [ ] **Business address** — a registered agent address works; Mercury accepts virtual/home addresses
- [ ] **Initial deposit** — Mercury has no minimum; have a small amount ready (~$100+)

> **Delaware + California note:** As a Delaware LLC doing business in California, you must be registered as a *foreign LLC* with the California Secretary of State and pay California's $800/yr minimum franchise tax. Bring both your Delaware docs *and* your CA registration when opening accounts.

---

### Banking pitfalls

- **Commingling personal and business funds** — even at $200k revenue, mixing accounts exposes your LLC's liability protection. Open the business account *before* your next client payment clears.
- **Using personal Zelle for business receipts** — most bank ToS prohibit using personal Zelle accounts for business transactions; Chase Business accounts have separate business Zelle, but verify terms before routing client payments there.
- **Assuming $250k FDIC coverage is enough** — at $200k revenue with lumpy consulting invoices, your balance can spike above $250k. Mercury's sweep network addresses this, but confirm the current sweep limit and which partner banks are active when you open.
- **Incomplete beneficial-ownership disclosure to the bank** — when you open your account, your bank is required under FinCEN's Customer Due Diligence rule to collect information on all individuals who own ≥25% of the LLC or exercise significant control. Provide accurate, consistent info for both founders. This is a *bank onboarding step*, not a separate government filing.

## Part 2: Insurance

### Policies this founder probably needs

---

**1. Technology E&O (Professional Liability / Tech E&O)**
- **Coverage:** $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate — the floor most healthcare clinic MSAs will specify
- **Approximate annual cost:** $1,500–$3,000/yr
- **Why you need it:** You're delivering software consulting *to healthcare organizations* — if your code, architecture advice, or implementation causes a clinic to lose revenue, miss appointments, or face a systems outage, they can and will sue. Healthcare clients carry high stakes and sophisticated legal teams. Most will require a certificate of insurance before the contract is signed.
- **Common deductible:** $1,000–$5,000 per claim; some carriers offer first-dollar defense

> **Tech E&O vs. generic E&O:** Make sure you're buying *Technology E&O*, not a generic professional liability policy. Tech E&O specifically covers software errors, system failures, and technology-service claims — exactly your exposure.

---

**2. Cyber Liability**
- **Coverage:** $1M per occurrence
- **Approximate annual cost:** $800–$2,000/yr (healthcare-adjacent work pushes you toward the higher end)
- **Why you need it:** Even though you don't store PHI, you almost certainly access clinic systems, cloud environments, or have clinic data flowing through your laptops and email. A ransomware hit on your machines, or a breach traced back to your credentials, could trigger a client claim. Cyber policies also cover your own incident response costs (forensics, notification, legal) — which easily exceed $50k on a modest incident.
- **Common deductible:** $2,500–$10,000

> Many carriers offer a bundled **Tech E&O + Cyber** policy for software firms — ask for a combined quote, as the premium is often 20–30% less than buying separately.

---

**3. General Liability (GL)**
- **Coverage:** $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate
- **Approximate annual cost:** $400–$800/yr
- **Why you need it:** Your client contracts almost certainly require it. GL covers third-party bodily injury and property damage — if you visit a clinic and something goes wrong, or a client alleges your work damaged their equipment. It's also the most commonly requested certificate in B2B vendor agreements.
- **Common deductible:** Often $0 (GL is typically first-dollar coverage)

---

**4. Workers' Compensation** *(future trigger — monitor now)*
- **Coverage:** California statutory limits (mandatory minimums set by state)
- **Approximate annual cost:** N/A today; ~$1,500–$3,000+/yr when you hire
- **Why this matters:** You have no employees today, so workers' comp is not currently required in California. However, **the moment you hire even one W-2 employee in California — including part-time — workers' comp is legally required from day one.** California has strict enforcement and personal liability for non-compliant owners. Put a note in your hiring checklist now.
- **Not applicable:** As LLC members actively working in the business, you can elect to exclude yourselves from workers' comp coverage in California.

---

**Policies you don't need yet:**
- ❌ **D&O** — no investors, no board; irrelevant at this stage
- ❌ **EPLI** — no employees
- ❌ **Commercial Property** — no leased/owned office space
- ❌ **Commercial Auto** — no company vehicles

### Insurance pitfalls

- **Skipping E&O because "you're just consulting"** — at $200k revenue, one client claiming your software advice caused $300k in lost billing is existential without coverage. Healthcare clients operate on thin margins and will pursue claims aggressively.
- **Assuming no PHI = no cyber exposure** — even a breach of your *own* systems (proposal docs, SOW files, email threads about a clinic's infrastructure) can trigger client notification obligations and legal costs that cyber insurance covers.
- **General Liability as a substitute for E&O** — GL does *not* cover professional errors, bad advice, or software failures. These are separate products. Founders sometimes buy only GL because it's cheaper, leaving their biggest actual exposure uninsured.
- **Underinsuring to save a few hundred dollars** — the difference between a $500k and $1M E&O limit is roughly $300–$500/yr. A single healthcare client dispute can easily hit seven figures. Buy the $1M/$2M limit from the start.

## 30-day action plan

1. **This week — Open the bank account:** Apply at Mercury online with your Delaware Certificate of Formation, CA Certificate of Authority, Operating Agreement, and EIN letter. Fund it and update all client payment instructions.

2. **Week 2 — Get insurance quotes:** Request quotes from at least two sources — Embroker or Hiscox for a bundled Tech E&O + Cyber quote, and Next Insurance or a local independent broker for comparison. Ask each for a **certificate of insurance** sample so you know it'll satisfy your client contracts.

3. **Week 3 — Bind your primary policies:** Bind Tech E&O + Cyber first (highest risk exposure), then GL. Most carriers let you get a certificate of insurance the same day. If you have a pending client contract requiring COIs, flag this as urgent.

4. **Week 4 — California compliance review:** Confirm your CA foreign LLC registration is current and the $800 franchise tax is calendared. Set a reminder: if you hire anyone in California, trigger a workers' comp policy *before* their first day of work.
