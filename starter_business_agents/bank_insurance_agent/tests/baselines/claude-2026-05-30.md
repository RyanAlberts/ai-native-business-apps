### Banking pitfalls

- **Commingling personal and business funds.** With two partners, the temptation to route reimbursements or distributions through personal accounts is high. Every dollar in and out should flow through the LLC account; distributions should be explicit transfers with a memo.
- **Zelle for business payments.** Chase and most banks prohibit or restrict Zelle use for business-to-business transactions in their ToS. Use ACH, wire, or a business payment platform (Stripe, Bill.com) instead.
- **Misunderstanding FDIC coverage at digital banks.** Mercury and Bluevine are not banks themselves — they hold deposits through partner banks. The sweep network extends effective FDIC coverage well beyond $250k, but it is **not** the same as a direct relationship with an FDIC-insured bank. At $200k revenue, you're likely fine, but confirm sweep details when you open.
- **Incomplete beneficial-ownership disclosure at account opening.** When the bank asks you to complete the FinCEN CDD (Customer Due Diligence) form, both members must be accurately listed. Omitting or understating a partner's ownership share is a compliance violation that can freeze your account. This is a **banking step**, not a separate government filing — just fill it out accurately when you open.

> 💡 **Bundle tip:** Many tech-focused insurers (Hiscox, Next Insurance) offer a **Tech E&O + GL bundle** specifically for software consultants. At your stage this is often better value than separate policies — ask for a combined quote.

**3. Cyber Liability**

- **Coverage limit:** $1M
- **Approximate annual cost:** $800–$2,000/yr (lower end because you don't store PHI)
- **Why you need it:** You don't store PHI, which meaningfully reduces your risk profile — but you likely have access credentials to clinic systems, email with clinic staff, and potentially client data on your laptops. A ransomware attack, a compromised laptop, or a phishing event that exposes a clinic's operational data could still trigger a claim. Healthcare clients are increasingly writing cyber requirements into vendor agreements.
- **Common deductible:** $1,000–$2,500

As two LLC members with no W-2 employees, you are currently exempt. If you bring on a part-time contractor who could be reclassified under California's AB5 test, that exposure appears quickly. Add this policy before your first hire, not after.

### Insurance pitfalls

- **Skipping E&O to save money early.** The logic "we're small, we won't get sued" fails in healthcare consulting specifically — clinics operate on thin margins, carry their own insurance, and their carriers will subrogate against you after a claim. One uninsured E&O event can dissolve the LLC.
- **Assuming your homeowner's or renter's policy covers business equipment.** Your laptops, external drives, and monitors used for client work are almost certainly excluded from personal policies once they're used commercially. A Tech E&O bundle often includes a small business property/equipment rider — confirm coverage for your hardware.
- **Not reading the MSA insurance requirements before binding.** Healthcare clients often specify required limits, additional insured endorsements, and waiver of subrogation clauses. Bind your policy *after* reviewing your first major client contract, not before — you may need to adjust limits or add endorsements.
- **Misclassifying future contractors.** California's AB5 law sets a high bar for independent contractor status. If you hire a contract developer in CA, get a workers' comp opinion before assuming they're excluded — getting this wrong means retroactive premiums and potential fines.

- **Why you need it:** Commercial auto covers the truck as a vehicle; it does NOT cover the cooking equipment, refrigeration units, or POS system inside it. A grease fire, equipment theft, or generator failure can idle you for weeks without this coverage.

**4. Workers' Compensation**

At $500–$1,500/yr, it's cheap protection. Bind it now before you scale.
- **Common deductible:** Usually $0 (first-dollar coverage); some policies offer large-deductible options to reduce premium

> **Florida note:** Workers' comp requirements are triggered by *total* employee count, including part-time. If you add one more person, you'll hit the 4-employee threshold and it becomes mandatory. Plan accordingly.
